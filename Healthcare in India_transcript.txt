RESEARCH BRIEF
----------------------------------------
Healthcare in India is a complex and multifaceted issue, impacting
millions across the nation. As of 2021, India had a healthcare
expenditure of approximately 3.6% of its GDP, among the lowest globally.
The World Health Organization rated India's healthcare system poorly,
citing insufficient public health spending, inadequate access to
services, and poor quality of care. A key debate centers around the
effectiveness of the Ayushman Bharat scheme, which aims to provide
health insurance to over 100 million families, yet faces challenges like
inefficiency and exclusion of marginalized communities. The COVID-19
pandemic brought to light the long-standing weaknesses in the healthcare
infrastructure, debunking the myth of India's robust health system.
There’s a growing emphasis on investment in both urban and rural
healthcare solutions, highlighting the disparity in access. The urban
healthcare system is often overwhelmed, while rural areas suffer from
underfunding and lack of resources. The conversation also includes the
role of private healthcare, which, although better equipped, drives up
costs and may leave the lower-income population behind. Recent
developments include the push for telemedicine and digital health
solutions as a means to improve access, especially in remote areas, with
significant investment flowing into health tech startups. Lesser-known
facts include the paradox of high healthcare costs coupled with a high
disease burden, especially from non-communicable diseases. Overall, the
Indian healthcare landscape is poised for transformative changes, but
significant challenges remain.
